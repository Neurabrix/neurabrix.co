Three different questions.

Doctors often ask only, “Will this train the model?” Privacy begins one step earlier: should identifiable data enter this vendor’s system at all?

Exposure

Uploading PII / patient data

A name is not the only name tag.

Deleting the patient’s name is not enough if the remaining details can still identify them.

🪪

Direct identifiers

  • Name, MRN, ABHA or insurance ID
  • Phone, email, address
  • Signatures and account numbers
📅

Indirect identifiers

  • Exact dates and precise location
  • Occupation, family details
  • Rare diagnosis + small community
🩻

Hidden in files

  • Headers, footers, QR codes, barcodes
  • Faces, tattoos, voice, screenshots
  • Filename and document metadata
🧩

Re-identification

  • Several “harmless” facts combined
  • External news or social posts
  • Unique clinical timelines

India · legal context

DPDP is phased—not a future excuse.

The framework is coming into force in stages. Build the safeguards now; do not wait for the last commencement date.

Where things stand on 10 August 2026

11 Aug 2023DPDP Act enacted

Creates India’s digital personal-data framework.

13 Nov 2025 · activeBoard-related provisions begin

Data Protection Board established; selected institutional provisions commenced.

13 Nov 2026Consent-manager provisions

A smaller next phase starts one year after notification.

13 May 2027Most operating duties begin

Core consent, data-fiduciary, safeguards, rights, breach, and penalty provisions commence.

Today: existing duties do not disappear. Medical records and health information are expressly sensitive personal data under the 2011 IT SPDI Rules, and professional confidentiality continues to matter.

What the DPDP operating model expects

1
Lawful, specified purposeDo not reuse clinical data for unrelated demos, research, or model improvement without a valid basis.
2
Specific, informed consent where relied uponClear notice; only data necessary for the stated purpose; withdrawal must be possible.
3
Clinic remains accountableA vendor is a processor, not an accountability escape hatch. Use a valid contract and verify controls.
4
Reasonable safeguardsMasking or tokenisation, access control, monitoring, logs, incident response, and processor clauses.
5
Accuracy, rights, and erasureSupport access, correction, grievance, and deletion when purpose ends—subject to legal retention duties.
6
Emergency is narrowImmediate threat to life or health can justify necessary processing; convenience, teaching, or a demo is not an emergency.

Claude public-sharing incident

“Anyone with the link” can become anyone.

In July 2026, publicly shared Claude pages appeared in Google and Bing results. Reports described sensitive material, including patient reports and clinical-trial data.

💬
01 · PRIVATEUser has a chat

Claude chats start private by default.

⌁
02 · SHAREPublic snapshot created

The share action makes a link that anyone holding it can view.

🌐
03 · DISCOVERLink reaches the public web

A forum, social post, archive, or other crawlable page can expose the URL.

🔎
04 · INDEXSearch amplifies access

Indexed pages become discoverable far beyond the intended recipient.

What it was notA breach exposing every private Claude chat. Reporting said the affected content had been intentionally shared.
What it demonstratesA public link is publication, not private messaging. Removing the attachment does not remove details repeated in the conversation or output.
Review Settings → Privacy → Shared chatsRevoke stale linksNever share patient-identifiable chatsAssume copies and archives may remain

Safer pattern

Summarising a pattern, not a person

A clinician wants help explaining a glucose trend.

  • Removes name, ID, exact dates, and rare context
  • Uploads only the relevant values
  • Checks the output; deletes it per policy
Avoid

“Quick second opinion” in a personal account

A full PDF discharge summary is pasted into a consumer chatbot.

📄 → 🌐
  • Name, MRN, address, and QR code remain
  • Training toggle is assumed to solve privacy
  • Chat link is posted to a doctors’ group
  • No contract, approval, audit trail, or deletion plan
Narrow exception

Immediate threat to life or health

Only the information necessary to respond to the emergency is processed.

🚑 → ⏱
  • Use the approved clinical pathway if available
  • Minimise data and record the necessity
  • Do not reuse later for teaching or model training
  • Report any accidental disclosure promptly

Dos and don’ts

Make the safe path the easy path.

✓ Do

01
Use the hospital-approved accountManaged workspace, written terms, access control, audit, retention, and incident route.
02
Minimise before uploadingPrefer a short de-identified extract over a full report, scan, recording, or screenshot.
03
Check every surfaceTraining preference, history, memory, retention, residency, human review, connectors, links, and exports.
04
Keep a human in chargeVerify clinical facts and citations. AI output does not transfer professional accountability.
05
Delete and documentFollow the approved retention rule; log the purpose, tool, data class, reviewer, and deletion.

× Don’t

01
Do not upload “just this once”Speed, curiosity, panel preparation, or convenience is not a legal basis or control.
02
Do not trust name removal aloneExact dates, faces, rare diagnoses, location, metadata, or combinations can re-identify.
03
Do not use a public link as a handoffIf a patient chat must move, use the approved clinical system—not a share URL.
04
Do not add unapproved connectorsBrowser extensions and plug-ins may become additional data recipients.
05
Do not mistake “enterprise” for approvalThe product still needs organisational review, a contract, configured controls, and a permitted workflow.

60-second upload gate

Can this patient data go in?

Four yeses before upload. One no means stop and use a safer route.

01

Approved purpose?

Direct care or another documented lawful purpose—not curiosity, teaching, demo, or convenience.

Must be yes
02

Approved system?

Institution-approved product, workspace, vendor contract, configuration, and accountable owner.

Must be yes
03

Minimum necessary?

Identifiers removed, hidden metadata checked, and only task-relevant facts included.

Must be yes
04

Controlled exit?

No public link; output verified; deletion, logging, and incident route are known.

Must be yes
If you cannot answer confidently, do not upload. Ask your hospital’s privacy, security, legal, or data-protection lead.

If data was uploaded or shared accidentally

Contain first. Do not conceal.

A practical first response

Use your organisation’s incident plan. These steps are a discussion aid, not a substitute for the designated response team.

Stop further disclosureUnshare the link, remove access, pause the connector, and do not forward screenshots.
Preserve the factsRecord tool, account type, data involved, recipients, timestamps, settings, links, and actions taken.
Notify internally nowEscalate to the hospital privacy/security/legal or DPO route; do not wait to prove harm.
Use the formal breach processLet the accountable team assess patient and regulator notification under applicable law and policy.
